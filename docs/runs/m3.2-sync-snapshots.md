# m3.2: incremental sync and the nightly snapshot

The second M3 task ([m3.0](m3.0-plan.md) §1). m3.1 reads every store in full and builds the catalog ([m3.1](m3.1-catalog-read.md)); m3.2 keeps it current: a small incremental read every 15 minutes, and a nightly snapshot at 02:00 that also finds products trashed or deleted outside ShelfSync. L1 is complete ([t9 §7](t9-l1-diagnostics.md)): both stores read in full (2,398 products each, about 2.5 s a page, pages up to 345 KB); PluginWay stalled for minutes twice on an evening (about 21:45–22:50 IST) and read cleanly the next morning, so **a store that stalls at night must not cost the night's snapshot**.

### What to build
1. **Settings:** `sync_interval_minutes` (default 15, 5–240) and `snapshot_time` (default `02:00`, `HH:MM` local) in `db/settings.py`, exposed through `GET`/`PUT /api/settings/app` beside the existing keys (Lead (proposed); the screens are m3.4/m3.5).
2. **Migration `0004_sync.sql`:**
   - `sync_state` gains the incremental watermark (`watermark_gmt`, the store's own `date_modified_gmt` text) and the last incremental's time, result and counts.
   - **Snapshots, stored as changes, not as copies** (Lead (proposed), for size: 30 days × 2 stores × 2,402 products as full copies would be about 144,000 rows, and every daily database backup copies them): `snapshot_night` (one row per store per night: the local night date, started, finished, result, products seen, trashed, not seen) and `snapshot_state` (store, WooCommerce id, status, slug, type, the downloads and `latest_version*` meta as JSON, a SHA-256 of that state, `first_night`, `last_night`). Each night a product whose state equals its latest row only moves that row's `last_night`; a changed product gets a new row. The dates a product can be restored to (APL-14, M5) are the nights it was seen, worked out from the two tables. Say if you find a simpler shape that keeps SYNC-03's Accept and the size small.
   - Retention: rows whose `last_night` is older than `snapshot_retention_days` are deleted, and so are their nights.
3. **The incremental read** (in `apply/catalog_read.py`, beside the full listing, same session rules):
   - `GET /products` with `modified_after=<watermark − 2 minutes>` as store GMT, `dates_are_gmt=true`, `status=any`, **no** `downloadable` filter (a product whose downloads were removed must still be seen), `orderby=id`, `order=asc`, `per_page=100`, the same `_fields`; the count check and every m3.1 guard apply.
   - The first incremental after a full read starts from the largest `modified_gmt` that read saved. The watermark only ever comes from the store's own dates, never the PC's clock, and moves only after a read that fully worked.
   - Saving: changed products are upserted; a product that was in the catalog and now has no downloads is kept with its downloads emptied (m3.3 reports it); a new non-downloadable product is ignored; a new downloadable one is linked as in m3.1. A repeat of the same incremental changes nothing.
   - **Unverified, to test on the test site:** whether `modified_after` includes its boundary, its precision, and whether editing only a download or only meta moves `date_modified_gmt` (SYNC-01's Accept depends on it). Record what you find.
4. **The nightly snapshot** (SYNC-03, SYNC-04):
   - At `snapshot_time` local, with catch-up at the next start or wake (the `db/daily.py` pattern); one night per store at most once it has worked.
   - `GET /products` with `include_status=any,trash` (V28), `orderby=id`, `order=asc`, `per_page=100`, `_fields=id,slug,status,type,date_modified_gmt,downloads,meta_data`, no `downloadable` filter. If the store refuses `include_status` or doesn't honour `any,trash`, fall back to two listings, `status=any` then `status=trash`, and say which was used. Check V28 with Context7 and the test site, and record it in 04-A.
   - For each variable product, `GET /products/{id}/variations` (same guards); more than 50 variable products on a store stops that store's snapshot and reports it (V30 says 0 today).
   - **The ID listing comes from the same read** (SYNC-04): a catalog product now `trash` is "trashed outside ShelfSync"; one missing entirely is "deleted outside ShelfSync". Expose both through the change-detection interface (item 6); m3.3 turns them into Cleanup issues.
   - **A stalled store doesn't cost the night** (L1's lesson): a failed or timed-out snapshot read is tried again later the same night (Lead (proposed): up to 2 more times, 30 minutes apart), and one store's failure never blocks the other. **Mind APL-12's auto-pause:** every failed request counts toward a store's 10 in a row, and one attempt can fail 4 times (the first try and 3 retries), so three night attempts could auto-pause a store that was only slow for an hour. Propose and implement a rule that keeps a slow evening from auto-pausing a store by itself while a store that is really down still is. State it, test it, and the analyst checks it.
   - Paused and auto-paused stores are skipped, and the night records why.
5. **The scheduler and Sync now:**
   - One scheduler in the runner's lifespan: an incremental every `sync_interval_minutes` while the app runs, and the snapshot at its time. Nothing overlaps: a run that is due while another runs waits for it. All reads go through m3.1's runner, busy slots and address guard.
   - `POST /api/sync` becomes **incremental** once every active store has had a full read that worked; the wizard's "Build catalog" asks for a full one explicitly (a `kind` of `full` or `incremental` in the body; the default chooses). SYNC-05.
   - `GET /api/sync` adds, per store, the last incremental and the last snapshot night (time, result, counts) and the next scheduled time of each.
   - Shutdown stops everything between requests, as in m3.1.
6. **Change detection for m3.3** (a small internal interface, no Cleanup yet): from an incremental save or a snapshot, the products that changed outside ShelfSync (downloads, slug, status, type) with their before and after, and the trashed and deleted ones. Pure functions plus the queries they need; tested; m3.3 consumes them.
7. **`GET /api/catalog/{id}/snapshots`:** the nights each of the item's store products can be restored to (dates only, local, newest first).
8. **The test-only launcher** ([11](../11-decisions.md) 2026-11-17, M3 Q1): `python -m testenv.launch` starts ShelfSync from source on a **throwaway data folder** with `testenv`'s transport and the test site's Read key, so the exit test sees test-stack results in the real screens. It lives only in `testenv/`, is never shipped, and **the app gains no setting, environment variable or flag for it**: inject through the existing seams (the client factory) by building the server from `testenv`. Say how, and prove the shipped app has no way in.
9. **The test stack:**
   - **V3:** through `SeedClient` (test-only, Read/Write pair), PUT a sample's full `downloads` array keeping the download `id`s, read it back, and record whether the ids survive (04-A V3). `WooClient` stays GET-only.
   - **`-m teststack`:** an incremental sees an edit made through `SeedClient` (and say whether a download-only edit moves `date_modified_gmt`); the snapshot sees `old-slider` as trashed; a product trashed through `SeedClient` shows as trashed outside ShelfSync after the next snapshot, then `seed` puts it back. You may run `check`, `seed` (never `--reset`) and these tests; nothing else on the site; don't start or change Laragon or moto.
10. **Tests:** a fake clock across 02:00, across a DST change (use a zone that has one), a missed night caught up at start and at wake, and a sleep across several nights (one catch-up, not several); the 2-minute overlap and a repeat changing nothing; the watermark moving only after success and never from the PC clock; a variation's downloads; more than 50 variable products; the `include_status` fallback; retention; paused and auto-paused stores skipped; a stalled store retried later the same night without blocking the other; the auto-pause rule of item 4; the scheduler never overlapping a run; Sync now incremental, Build catalog full; no non-GET; nothing secret in any log line (the m3.1 never-logged pattern); the launcher can't be reached from the shipped app.
